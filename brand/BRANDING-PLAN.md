# Evan Fischell Consulting — Brand Development Plan

*A plan to develop the brand identity — not the finished identity itself. It sets strategy, locked and open decisions, visual direction, and a phased production plan. The finished logo, site, and deck come out of executing this plan (the deck is a separate, later conversation).*

*Decisions locked in the latest round are marked ✅. Still-open items are gathered in §7.*

---

## 0. Strategic context — build to be absorbed

The near-term goal is **not** a splashy public launch. Evan is positioning the venture to be **absorbed by a larger firm before it ever goes fully public**. That reframes the whole brand brief:

- **Two audiences, not one.** The health-system buyer (CDIO/CIO/CTO/CFO) *and* the **potential acquirer** — health-IT services firms, consultancies, and platform companies evaluating whether to buy the capability.
- **The brand must read as a credible, differentiated, absorbable asset** — professional and firm-grade, not a personal blog; sharp and legible, not over-branded or hype-driven. An acquirer should look at it and see something clean to fold in, with a defensible angle.
- **Priority order shifts:** a tight core identity and a **capabilities one-pager / private narrative** matter most; a full public marketing site matters least (a lean, credible holding presence is enough until an acquisition path resolves).
- **Founder-forward is fine and useful** — at this stage Evan's judgment *is* the asset an acquirer is buying — but the identity should be firm-grade so it survives being absorbed under a new parent.

## 1. Brand strategy

### Positioning statement

> For health-system executives — the CEOs and COOs worn down by the inexplicable complexity, cost, and delay of operational-technology vendor implementations, and the technology leaders who own that stack — **Evan Fischell Consulting** replaces the sprawl with right-sized systems the health system owns and runs, delivered in weeks. The engine is healthcare-focused software engineering, native to the agentic-engineering era. Unlike platform vendors whose incentive is more integration and more lock-in, we build for a clean hand-off. *You're not buying AI, and you're not buying a technology pitch — you're buying an end to the complexity.*

### Messaging pillars

Four pillars, each traceable to the pitch. Everything we publish should ladder up to one of them.

1. **It shouldn't be hard.** *(The tagline — see §2D.)* We end the inexplicable complexity — the chaotic, duplicative, over-engineered vendor sprawl — starting with operational technology, where the pain and the ROI are clearest. *(Internal shorthand: "rationalize the stack" — retired from external use; too technical for the executive reader.)*
2. **Software engineering, not an AI pitch.** Healthcare-focused engineering native to the agentic-engineering era. Right-sized, owned systems at a fraction of the cost and complexity — deterministic where that's simplest, embedded agents where they earn it. Built to scale, delivered as something you own.
3. **Secure & compliant by construction.** Built to operate inside a health system's constraints from day one — not retrofitted.
4. **Proof in weeks, not decks.** A real delivery pipeline plus a working prototype against a real need by week 4–6; replace the first product or roadmap the cleanup from there.

### Target audience

- **The frustrated executive — CEO / COO.** ✅ **The primary target.** They've watched vendor implementation after vendor implementation balloon in cost and time with no explanation anyone will give them straight — and they're done accepting "that's just how health IT is." Speak to them in business language: cost, time, accountability, ownership. **Never tech-first.**
- **Technology owner — CDIO / CIO / CTO.** The validator and often the sponsor; owns the strategy and feels the sprawl directly (failure mode #3 is their story). Technical depth surfaces here — below the cover, not on it.
- **Economic buyer — CFO / VP Finance.** Feels the budget overruns and the pay-a-lot-for-something-small waste; responds to cost-out and ownership.
- **Day-to-day owners — VP/Director of Applications, Enterprise Architecture, Digital.** Own the operational tech stack and the vendor relationships being rationalized.
- **Acquirer (per §0) — corp-dev / leadership at a larger health-IT firm, consultancy, or platform** evaluating the capability as an acquisition. They read the same differentiation, plus "is this cleanly absorbable and defensible?"

### Brand personality & voice

**Personality:** secure, rigorous, compliant, modern, pragmatic — **credible, not hypey.** An engineering firm's temperament, not a marketing shop's.

**Voice principles:**
- **Executive-plain, never tech-first.** ✅ The first reader is a CEO/COO who can't get a straight answer about why the last implementation took eighteen months. Lead with cost, time, accountability, and ownership in plain business language. The engineering is our credential, not our vocabulary — technical depth surfaces for the CDIO, never on the cover.
- **Software engineering forward, AI in the background.** ✅ Lead with the engineering and the outcome. Say **"agentic engineering"** as the discipline and **"embedded agents"** as one of its tools. Avoid "AI," "frontier AI," "AI-powered." The buyer isn't buying AI; they're buying healthcare software engineering that's native to this era — which is *why* we're fast, not the thing being sold.
- **Specific over sweeping.** Name the failure mode, name the week, name the deliverable. Concreteness is the credibility.
- **Plain and declarative.** Short sentences. Say what we do and what we don't.
- **Calm authority.** No exclamation-point energy, no "revolutionary," no "transform your enterprise." The confidence comes from precision.
- **Honest about trade-offs.** We'll tell a client when their vendor spend is fine, when deterministic beats agentic, when to roadmap instead of rip-and-replace. That candor is a differentiator.
- **Positive framing only.** ✅ (Evan, 2026-07-02) No negative terms about ourselves or others — no "trap," "dependency," "lock-in-avoidance" self-descriptions, no deny-a-sin constructions. State what we are: fast, real results, solving problems and delivering ROI as the business model. (The problem section describes the buyer's lived pain as a market pattern — that stays; this rule governs how we talk about our own motives and other companies.)

## 3. Visual identity direction

*Direction, not final art. The goal: fuse **trust/compliance** with **modern/engineering**, grounded in **healthcare credibility**, while staying clean and un-hypey — and legible enough to sit cleanly inside an acquirer's brand later.*

### Typography — ✅ locked

- **IBM Plex Sans** ✅ — chosen from a four-face specimen comparison (vs. Inter, Space Grotesk, Archivo). Engineered heritage, quietly technical, distinctive numerals, light weight holds up; free/open license (no font-licensing baggage for an acquirable brand).
- **Weights in use:** 600 (headlines, wordmark lead), 500 (subheads), 400 (body), 300 (the "Consulting" in the lockup; fine print).
- **Mono accent:** **IBM Plex Mono** for technical callouts and code — the matching sibling. Use sparingly.

### Logo / wordmark — ✅ locked

- **Lockup (T1):** ✅ **"Evan Fischell"** in Plex Sans 600 + **"Consulting"** in 300, with an **amber period** as the single color gesture. The name carries the weight; the descriptor recedes (so the mark survives if the descriptor ever changes — relevant to absorption). The period reads "statement, not slogan."
- **Monogram:** ✅ **"EF." plate** (typographic EF + amber period on an ink plate) — chosen as the *for-now* mark; **explicitly provisional**, Evan expects to revisit. The M1 "resolve" concept (tangled lines → clean bars) stays on file as the likely upgrade candidate.
- Produce clean single-color and reversed (knockout) versions so it works on a one-pager, a deck, and eventually inside an acquirer's collateral.

### Color palette — ✅ locked

**The token system (confirmed):**

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Ink 900 | `#0B1926` | Deepest surfaces — deck backgrounds, footers |
| Ink 800 | `#0F2233` | **Primary brand color** — wordmark, headlines, dark surfaces |
| Slate 600 | `#34505F` | Body text on light, secondary UI |
| Mist 300 | `#9FB0BC` | Muted text on dark, hairlines, captions |
| Paper 50 | `#F4F7F9` | Light backgrounds — documents, web |
| Amber 500 | `#E8912A` | **The signature.** Accents only: period, rule, one bar — ≤10% of any surface |
| Amber 600 | `#C1731A` | Amber as text on light (contrast-safe); hover states |
| Amber 50 | `#FBEEDC` | Warm tint for callout backgrounds on light |

**Usage rules:** navy and greys do all structural work; amber never fills large areas and is never body text; on light surfaces amber text uses 600, not 500; **no second hue** — if a diagram truly needs one, a desaturated slate variant, never teal.

*Selection history:*

Four colorblind-safe candidate palettes have been drafted and hosted for review (with a built-in deuteranopia/protanopia/tritanopia simulator). All are anchored in a deep blue/ink (trust + compliance) with accents separated from the base by **lightness** (so they stay distinct under color-vision deficiency), plus clean architectural greys and an off-white base.

- **A — Ink & ember** (navy + warm amber): safest and most distinct; a touch of warmth for healthcare credibility. **Recommended default.**
- **B — Deep sea** (ink + luminous teal): cooler, more frontier/engineering.
- **C — Blueprint** (ink + teal + amber): a two-accent *system* — useful for the before/after "sprawl → clean stack" diagrams.
- **D — Graphite & iris** (graphite + blue-violet): most distinctive/modern; the accent is the one to scrutinize under the simulator.

**Recommendation (updated after the competitive scan — see `COMPETITIVE-PALETTE-SCAN.md`):** adopt **A** — deep ink navy + warm amber — and **resist adding a teal/cyan second accent.** The 20-firm scan found that navy bases cluster tightly (credibility, but crowded — our ink sits right on Chartis, Impact Advisors, and McKinsey), and that **teal/cyan is the field's single most over-used accent** (Chartis, Health Catalyst, MEDITECH, Cognizant, Nordic all use it). Amber is the deliberate break — warmer and more human than the pack, and **no health-IT advisory firm owns it.** So: lean into the ink-dark navy, make the golden amber the ownable signature (kept distinct from the bright RCM oranges of Optum/Waystar), and prefer a **restrained navy + amber + grey-ramp system** over a second hue. If diagrams truly need a second accent, use a muted slate or desaturated sage — *not* bright teal. *Design intent:* mostly ink + white + grey (rigor, trust, compliance) with one disciplined warm accent; it should look like a security-grade engineering firm, not a generic AI startup. Palette selection is Evan's to confirm — see §7.

### Imagery & graphic style

- **Direction:** clean, structural, systems-oriented. Diagrams over stock photos. Architecture-and-flow visuals (pipelines, before/after stack maps, decoupling diagrams) are on-brand *because they are literally what we sell*.
- **Signature motif:** "order out of chaos" — tangled vendor sprawl → clean, rationalized stack — reused across one-pager, deck, and site.
- **Avoid:** glowing-brain / neural-network / robot-hand imagery (also keeps AI in the background); overused "digital hospital" stock; anything hype-adjacent. Photography, if used, should be spare and human (Evan; real settings), not clip-art futurism.

## 4. Voice & tone notes (applied)

- **Do:** lead with the specific problem and the concrete mechanic; use numbers and weeks; state what we *don't* do; be candid about trade-offs; talk about the engineering and the outcome.
- **Don't:** use "revolutionary / transform / unlock / supercharge / next-gen"; over-promise; agent-wash.
- **Lexicon — ✅ locked:** use **"agentic engineering"** (the discipline) and **"embedded agents"** (one tool among many). Say **"software engineering."** Minimize or omit **"AI"**; avoid **"frontier AI," "AI-powered," "AI-driven."**
- **Reading level & format:** short paragraphs, scannable structure, mobile-first. Every asset should survive being read on a phone.
- **Signature lines, by register:**
  - *Executive-facing (CEO/COO — frustration and relief):* **"It shouldn't be hard."** (the tagline — amber period) / "Delivered in weeks — and it's yours." / "Proof in weeks, not decks." / "An end to the complexity no one can explain." / **"Solving problems and delivering ROI is our business model."** (amber period — the abundance-confidence pillar, Evan 2026-07-02: fast + real results → never worried about the next engagement; **"10–100×" is technical-register only**, shared/exec surfaces say "we are fast"; state the pillar POSITIVELY — see voice rule below)
  - *Technical-validator-facing (CDIO/CIO — below the cover):* "Right-sized, not platform-sized." / "Deterministic where it's simpler, embedded agents where they earn it." / "You own it — supported as much as you want."
  - *Retired from external use:* "Rationalize the stack" (internal shorthand only — too technical). / "Built for exit, not lock-in" (reads one-foot-out-the-door; use the ownership-plus-support framing instead — Evan, 2026-07-02). / "Vendor target" / "target vendor" in any register (too technical, too hostile — say "the vendor(s) in question," "the first product"). / "Working software" in the **executive** register (name the outcome: spending reduced, complexity reduced, problems solved — fine in technical material). / "Maximal project" (unclear — say the real idea positively). / "We don't need to trap you" and all negative self-descriptions (see the positive-framing voice principle).

## 8. Light competitive/positioning awareness

*Included only where it sharpens differentiation — the specific angle stays the star. An acquirer reads these same contrasts as "what's defensible here."*

- **Big consultancies & health-IT advisory firms** (Accenture/Deloitte/Optum-advisory, Epic-services shops) sell *assessments, roadmaps, and staff augmentation* — strategy artifacts, then large billable implementations. **Our wedge:** working software and a real prototype by week 4–6, and a hand-off you own — proof, not a maturity model.
- **Healthcare-"AI" point vendors** mostly sell *another platform* (ambient scribing, prior-auth, RCM copilots). **Our wedge:** we're explicitly *anti-platform-vendor* and we don't sell AI — we reduce the platform count and the lock-in, and we build deterministic-or-agentic by judgment.
- **Generic "AI transformation" consultancies** lead with hype and model names. **Our wedge:** software-engineering-forward, AI-in-the-background voice, plus deep specificity about the operational-tech failure modes. Credibility, not buzz.

Through-line: **we build and hand off exact-fit systems in weeks, our incentive is a rational stack (not more integration), we're secure/compliant, and we sell engineering — not AI.** Keep that front and center; the landscape note only makes it land harder.
