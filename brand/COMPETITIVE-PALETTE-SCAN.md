# Competitive palette scan — where EFC's navy + amber sits

*A scan of the brand colors of 20 leading healthcare-technology and consulting firms, and how Evan Fischell Consulting's recommended **Palette A (Ink #0F2233 + Amber #E8912A)** compares. Hexes were sampled from official logo vectors, published brand guidelines, or live production CSS where possible; lower-confidence items are flagged. Companion visual (with a color-vision simulator): see the hosted comparison page.*

---

## The headline

**The base blends in on purpose; the accent is what sets us apart.** Deep blue/navy is the single largest cluster in this field — so a navy base reads as instant credibility, but it won't differentiate on its own. The reflexive *accent* in healthcare tech is cyan/teal (six-plus firms reach for it). **EFC's warm amber deliberately breaks from that** — it reads warmer and more human than the pack of cold-digital blues, while the ink-dark navy keeps the seriousness. Our nearest palette cousin is ZS Associates (indigo + orange); everyone else lands somewhere clearly distinct.

Net: **navy + amber threads a real gap** — the credibility of the advisory/analytics blues, minus the cyan cliché, without tipping into the bright consumer-orange of the RCM crowd.

## How EFC Palette A compares

**EFC Palette A:** Ink `#0F2233` · Slate `#34505F` · Mist `#9FB0BC` · Paper `#F4F7F9` · **Amber `#E8912A`**

**1. The navy base signals credibility but is near-identical to the advisory pack.**
Our Ink `#0F2233` sits right on top of The Chartis Group `#0E2A4B`, Impact Advisors `#10325C`, and McKinsey `#051C2C`. That's the point — it borrows the "serious, trusted advisor" read. The upside: EFC's ink is genuinely *dark* (closer to McKinsey's authority-navy than to the friendlier mid-blues of Huron `#00568C`, Innovaccer `#0060D6`, or Health Catalyst `#005CB7`), which keeps it from looking like a generic SaaS blue.

**2. The amber accent is the real differentiator — because the field's reflex is cyan.**
Chartis, Cognizant, Health Catalyst, MEDITECH, and Nordic all pair their blue/navy with a teal/cyan accent. By pairing navy with **warm amber instead**, EFC immediately reads warmer and less coldly-digital than the advisory and analytics firms it will be compared to. This is the single strongest move in the palette.

**3. Warm-on-dark isn't unique — but our *tone* of warm is well-separated.**
Optum `#FF612B`, Waystar `#FF6900`, and NextGen `#DC6B2F` already own bright orange. EFC's `#E8912A` is a more **golden, muted amber** — it reads premium and serious rather than consumer-disruptor. Good separation, as long as we keep the amber golden (not sliding toward the RCM red-orange).

**4. Closest cousin: ZS Associates (indigo `#2E3192` + orange `#F7941E`).**
ZS is the only firm here with the same navy-ish + warm-spark structure. But ZS is life-sciences/pharma commercial analytics — a different lane from health-system operational-tech rationalization — and its indigo + brighter orange are distinct enough. Worth knowing, not a real collision.

**5. Nobody in the direct-competitor set (health-IT advisory) owns amber.** That's open ground.

## Recommendation update (from this scan)

1. **Keep navy + amber — the pairing is well-differentiated for our lane.** Confirmed as the direction.
2. **Lean into the ink-dark navy.** `#0F2233` (or slightly deeper) reads as McKinsey-grade authority and separates us from the friendly mid-blues. Don't lighten it toward a SaaS blue.
3. **Make the amber the ownable signature** — a specific golden amber (`#E8912A` is a good anchor), kept distinct from the bright RCM red-oranges.
4. **Reconsider a teal/cyan second accent.** This is the one change from the earlier plan: teal is the *most crowded* accent in the field (six-plus firms). Adding it for diagrams (as Palette C proposed) would make us look like Chartis / Health Catalyst / MEDITECH / Cognizant / Nordic. Two better options:
   - **(Preferred) A restrained monochrome-plus-amber system** — navy, amber, and a full grey ramp only. In a field of busy multi-hue accent systems, disciplined restraint would actually stand out and reinforce the "rigorous, not hypey" personality.
   - If diagrams genuinely need a second hue, use a **muted slate-blue or a desaturated sage/olive** — deliberately *not* bright teal.
5. **Avoid, as identity colors:** bright cyan/teal (cliché), purple (owned by Accenture/athenahealth), and green (owned by Deloitte/BCG).

*Bottom line: Palette A is a good, defensible choice. Its base is intentionally conventional; its warmth is the differentiator. The sharpest upgrade from this scan is to resist the teal reflex and let navy + amber + disciplined greys carry the whole system.*
