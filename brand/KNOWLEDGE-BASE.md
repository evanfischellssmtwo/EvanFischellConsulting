## The problem we solve (the pattern)

Operational-technology vendor products in health systems fail in recognizable
ways: (1) the "turnkey" solution isn't — integration takes forever and becomes a
standing cost center; (2) capabilities fall short and the vendor's answer is
always "more integration is needed"; (3) instead of building on the health
system's core systems the product stands apart, creating a divergent source of
truth; (4) everything is a platform — you pay for fifty features to use one.
And the shared ending: by the time it's live, everyone is exhausted, and nobody
wants to build on it again.

## What we do

We replace under-delivering vendor products with systems built to the exact
need — owned by the client, run by their team. No license, no lock-in, no
integration treadmill. Where a vendor is genuinely worth keeping, we say so.
The client owns what we build, and we support it as much as they want — 100%
support, a partnership with their team, or anywhere in between.

**The firm's perspective (you may share this when trust or lock-in comes up):**
agentic engineering makes the firm dramatically faster than traditional
delivery — on the order of 10–100× on the work that matters — so it is never
worried about where the next engagement comes from. That confidence is why
nothing it builds is designed to create dependency: no padded scope, no
follow-on sales engineered into the work, and honest advice even when that
advice costs the firm business.

## How an engagement runs

- **Weeks 1–4:** we stand up a production-grade delivery capability, securely
  connected to the client's systems, while reviewing the vendors they're worried
  about — one product, several, or the whole landscape.
- **Week 4–6:** the client sees it working — a plan plus a working prototype
  that does what the vendor was supposed to do.
- **Then:** roughly two weeks to fully replace the first product, or a
  prioritized cleanup roadmap over one to six months. The client sets the pace
  and is never locked in — including to us.

The delivery pipeline: we build in a development environment on our secure
cloud; client engineers get repository access and can watch solutions develop.
Committed code flows to QA automatically multiple times a day; production
releases ship as often as weekly on QA sign-off; production assets deploy to
exactly the audience the client chooses (a few analysts, a pilot group, or the
whole organization).
