## The problem

Large health systems have almost universally accumulated a chaotic sprawl of technology investments. Executives feel it before anyone can explain it: implementations sold as a quarter run for years, budgets balloon, promised value never lands — and nobody inside or outside the building can give the CEO a straight answer about *why it's this complicated*. The clearest, most fixable damage is in the **operational technology stack** — and especially in the **small and mid-size vendor products** that pile up across departments. These products share a recognizable set of failure modes:

**1. The turnkey solution isn't.** What was sold as plug-and-play disappears into an "integration" phase that runs well beyond budget in both time and money. What was sold as a quarter becomes a year; what was scoped as a project becomes a standing cost center.

**2. It falls short of what was promised.** The vendor promises a lot up front. After all the integration work, some of the capabilities that justified the purchase take longer to arrive than expected — and the explanation is often the same: *"more integration is needed."* The goalposts move, and the bill grows.

**3. A divergent source of truth.** Instead of building on the capabilities of your core systems, the product stands entirely apart — worse than duplicative, it creates a second version of the truth that diverges from the systems you already trust. And it often cuts against the higher-level technology strategy: when the CDIO finally hears about it, the realization lands that Epic (or another core vendor) already does this, or should have been the path in the first place.

**4. Everything is a platform.** The system pays a premium for a platform capable of many things when the clear ROI is in one small aspect. The key piece — your actual reason for purchase — may eventually work well, but after going over budget just to integrate the minimum, you're now paying for a platform full of features you will never use, and the one value-adding feature is irrationally complicated to configure inside it. It may still be net-positive ROI — but everyone involved knows it's wasteful.

And the four failure modes share an ending: **by the time it's live, everyone is exhausted.** The platform sounded great in the pitch — the real value was everything you'd build on top of it — but after the integration slog and one hard-won pilot, nobody in the building ever wants to build on it again. The platform's premise dies at go-live.

The pattern underneath all of it: **the system is carrying cost, complexity, and dependency that far exceed the value actually being delivered.** That gap is the opportunity.

## What we do

We bring a **secure, compliant agentic-engineering platform** to the problem and use it to deliver the *exact* system a health system needs — no more, no less — and then hand it over as something they own and can run.

Two commitments define the approach:

**Right-sized, not platform-sized.** Where a vendor sells a broad platform and charges for capability you'll never configure, we build the specific capability that carries the ROI. A custom system that meets the exact need currently (poorly) met by the vendor — while remaining capable of scaling when you actually want to scale.

**We build the simplest thing that's correct.** Agentic engineering lets us produce a wide range of solutions quickly, and we choose by fit, not by fashion. Often the right answer is a fully deterministic system, and that's what we build. When live agents genuinely earn their place, we can drop in **embedded agents** — real agents in the product, not chatbots bolted on. Embedded agents are simply one of the many tools agentic engineering puts within easy reach; they're never the point.

Security and compliance are not a feature bullet; they are the precondition for doing any of this inside a health system. The platform is built to connect to a customer's systems and operate under that constraint from the first day.

## How we engage

The engagement is designed so that within weeks a customer has both a working delivery capability *and* concrete proof against a real target — not a slide deck.

**Weeks 0–4 — Stand up the pipeline, in parallel with diligence.**
- In **2–4 weeks** we establish a real product-delivery pipeline: a dev environment, a QA pipeline, acceptance testing, and a high-reliability production environment.
- How that pipeline runs day to day: we build in a development environment on **our secure cloud**, and your engineers get repository access to watch the solutions develop. Committed code flows to **QA automatically, multiple times a day**; we can provide QA independently or with your team participating. Production releases ship **as often as weekly** on QA sign-off, and production assets are deployed to exactly the audience you choose — a few engineers and analysts, a pilot group, or the entire organization.
- Concurrently, we connect our secure agentic-engineering platform to the customer's systems and begin diligence — either a **full landscape evaluation (2–4 weeks)** or a **focused effort** on one or more vendor products the customer wants to address first.

**Week 4 (focused) or Week 6 (open-ended review) — Deliver a plan plus a working prototype.**
- We deliver a **plan** and **at least one functional development prototype** of a custom system that meets the exact needs currently met by the vendor(s) in question — built to scale, not just to demo.

**From there — Decouple, or roadmap.**
- You are roughly **two weeks from fully replacing** the identified vendor product(s), **or**
- you hold a **clear, prioritized roadmap** to clean up your vendor dependencies over the next **1–6 months**, depending on ambition.

The ceiling is set by appetite, not capability — we can do anything from a single surgical replacement to a broad rationalization program.

## What we are *not*

- **Not an AI pitch.** We rarely lead with "AI." The work is healthcare software engineering; agentic engineering is how we do it fast and cheap. You're buying the engineering and the outcome, not a model.
- **Not selling chatbots.** A chat box is not a strategy.
- **Not "agent-washing."** We don't turn everything into an agent to look modern. Embedded agents appear only where they're the simplest correct tool; when deterministic wins, deterministic wins.
- **Not a platform vendor.** We're the opposite trade: we exist to *reduce* the number of over-engineered platforms you're paying for, not to add another one.
- **Not a "turnkey" product.** One size does not fit all — and with the technology available today, it doesn't have to. Everything we deliver is built to your need, not to a market's average.
- **Not a rip-and-replace-everything crusade** *(executive framing: not disruption for its own sake)*. We work *with* your existing investments — we can integrate with anything — and some vendor spend is net-positive and worth keeping; we say so. We never inflate scope to create after-work.
